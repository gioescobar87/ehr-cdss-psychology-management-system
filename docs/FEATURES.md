# Feature Reference

## 1. Overview

The Clinical Psychology Management System integrates patient management, clinical documentation, psychological care workflows, administrative coordination, and deterministic diagnostic decision support.

This document describes the functional areas of the application, the intended behavior of the main workflows, and the access-control considerations associated with clinical information.

Feature availability depends on the current implementation and configuration. A described workflow should not be interpreted as fully implemented or validated without corresponding application and test evidence.

## 2. User Roles and Access Control

The application uses role-based access control to regulate access to functionality and sensitive information.

Users are authenticated before accessing protected application resources. The backend evaluates permissions and applicable business rules for protected operations.

### 2.1 Administrator

The administrator manages system-level functionality according to assigned permissions.

Responsibilities may include:

- User administration.
- Role and permission configuration.
- Management of system catalogs.
- Administrative configuration of supported modules.

### 2.2 Coordinator

The coordinator oversees authorized clinical and administrative workflows.

Responsibilities may include:

- Viewing patients within the scope of assigned permissions.
- Assigning and reassigning patients to psychologists.
- Monitoring clinical appointments and sessions.
- Accessing coordination reports.
- Reviewing operational information needed to coordinate services.

### 2.3 Psychologist

The psychologist manages clinical workflows for assigned patients.

Responsibilities may include:

- Accessing assigned patient records.
- Maintaining authorized clinical documentation.
- Recording clinical sessions and evaluations.
- Managing relevant appointment workflows.
- Reviewing diagnostic suggestions generated by the CDSS.
- Confirming or selecting a diagnosis according to professional judgment.

The exact capabilities of each role depend on the application's configured permissions.

## 3. Patient Management

The patient management module supports the registration and maintenance of patient information.

### Core Responsibilities

- Registering patient records.
- Retrieving patient information.
- Updating authorized patient information.
- Validating required data.
- Associating patients with the relevant clinical workflows.
- Enforcing authorization before protected patient operations.

Patient records contain sensitive information and must not be accessible solely because a user knows a patient identifier.

### Assignment-Aware Access

Psychologists are restricted to patients assigned to them under the application's authorization rules.

Authorized coordinators can oversee assignments and perform permitted assignment changes.

Assignment changes should retain historical information to support traceability.

## 4. Clinical Records and Medical History

The clinical record functionality organizes information relevant to psychological care.

Depending on the implemented workflows, this includes:

- Clinical history.
- Relevant background information.
- Documented clinical observations.
- Clinical notes and supporting information.
- Links to evaluations, sessions, and related records.

The application separates structured information from stored clinical documents where appropriate.

Access to clinical history must follow the same authorization principles applied to other sensitive patient information.

## 5. Appointment Management

Appointment management supports the scheduling and tracking of clinical appointments.

The intended workflow includes:

- Registering appointments.
- Associating appointments with patients and responsible professionals.
- Recording appointment dates and relevant information.
- Tracking appointment status.
- Supporting coordination and operational reporting.

The application must validate the relationships between appointments, patients, and authorized users.

Appointment status should accurately reflect the relevant workflow. For example, a scheduled appointment, a completed appointment, and a canceled appointment represent different operational outcomes.

## 6. Clinical Sessions

The clinical session module records activities associated with psychological care.

Responsibilities may include:

- Creating session records.
- Associating sessions with patients and responsible psychologists.
- Recording relevant clinical documentation.
- Linking sessions to the corresponding clinical history.
- Maintaining appropriate session status.
- Supporting operational reporting.

Clinical session records must be protected against unauthorized viewing and modification.

The exact relationship between appointments and sessions depends on the implemented business rules. Reporting logic should use the actual status and completion behavior implemented by the application.

## 7. Psychological Evaluations and Tests

The system supports the management of psychological evaluation workflows and a catalog of psychological tests.

The catalog may include information such as:

- Test name.
- Description.
- Evaluation objective.
- Application instructions.
- Relevant considerations.
- Active or inactive status.

The catalog helps organize available instruments and their associated information.

The availability of a test in the catalog does not imply that the application automatically scores, interprets, or clinically validates its results. Those capabilities must be documented separately if implemented.

Evaluation records and their results must remain accessible only to appropriately authorized users.

## 8. Clinical Document Management

The application integrates with Amazon S3 to store clinical documents separately from structured relational data.

The intended document workflow includes:

- Associating documents with relevant clinical records.
- Uploading documents through authorized application operations.
- Retrieving documents through authorized access paths.
- Restricting direct access to private storage objects.
- Applying appropriate storage and encryption controls.

The backend and AWS permissions must prevent unauthorized users from retrieving clinical documents.

Document storage must not rely on public bucket access or unrestricted object URLs.

## 9. Diagnostic Catalog

The diagnostic catalog organizes available diagnoses into diagnostic categories.

The catalog supports:

- Browsing available diagnostic categories.
- Retrieving active diagnostic entries.
- Displaying diagnostic names and descriptions.
- Identifying diagnoses using configured classification codes.
- Selecting a diagnosis during the appropriate clinical workflow.

The catalog and the diagnostic rule engine serve different purposes.

The catalog defines which diagnoses can be presented to users. The rule engine evaluates modeled criteria to determine whether a diagnostic suggestion is supported by the documented information.

A diagnosis being available in the catalog does not automatically imply that a rule exists for that diagnosis.

## 10. Clinical Decision Support System

The CDSS provides deterministic diagnostic suggestions based on explicitly modeled criteria.

Its purpose is to support professional review and diagnostic concordance evaluation, not to replace the psychologist's clinical judgment.

### 10.1 Rule-Based Evaluation

The logical rule model consists of:

1. Diagnostic categories.
2. Diagnoses associated with those categories.
3. Diagnostic decision trees.
4. Nodes within decision trees.
5. Rules associated with applicable nodes.
6. Criteria evaluated against documented clinical information.

The actual evaluation sequence depends on the implemented rule engine and its persistence model.

### 10.2 Suggestion Workflow

The intended interaction follows these steps:

1. The professional documents the relevant clinical information.
2. The application evaluates the applicable diagnostic rules.
3. When the required conditions match, the system generates a suggestion.
4. The suggestion is presented for professional review.
5. The psychologist confirms the suggested diagnosis or selects another diagnosis.
6. The system retains the information required by the implemented clinical workflow and concordance evaluation.

A suggestion must not be treated as an automatically confirmed professional diagnosis.

### 10.3 Deterministic Behavior

The CDSS evaluates explicit conditions and rules. It does not use machine learning, natural language processing, fuzzy logic, or probabilistic inference to produce its diagnostic suggestions.

The same applicable rule definitions and equivalent input conditions should produce reproducible evaluation results.

The output remains dependent on the quality of the documented information, the completeness of the modeled criteria, and the correctness of the implementation.

### 10.4 No-Match Behavior

When the documented information does not satisfy an applicable rule, the system should not invent a diagnostic suggestion.

The absence of a suggestion does not establish that a patient has no disorder or that further clinical assessment is unnecessary.

### 10.5 Professional Responsibility

Diagnostic suggestions are intended to assist clinical reasoning.

The psychologist remains responsible for reviewing the available information, considering the broader clinical context, and determining the appropriate diagnosis.

The application should not present deterministic rule matches as proof of diagnostic validity or clinical effectiveness.

## 11. Diagnostic Concordance Evaluation

Diagnostic concordance compares the system's original suggestion with the diagnosis subsequently confirmed by the psychologist.

For a completed evaluation in which both values are available:

- **Concordant:** The original system suggestion matches the professional's confirmed diagnosis.
- **Discordant:** The original system suggestion does not match the professional's confirmed diagnosis.

The comparison must use the original suggestion, not a value overwritten by the professional's selection.

Cases without a valid system suggestion or a confirmed professional diagnosis require explicit handling in the evaluation methodology.

Concordance is a measure of agreement between the system and the professional. It is not, by itself, a measure of diagnostic accuracy because it does not establish an independent clinical reference standard.

Any reported results should identify the evaluation period, sample, inclusion criteria, and methodology used.

## 12. Patient Assignment Management

Patient assignment functionality establishes the relationship between patients and responsible psychologists.

The assignment workflow is intended to support:

- Assigning a patient to a psychologist.
- Reassigning a patient when authorized.
- Restricting access according to the current assignment.
- Maintaining assignment history.
- Recording the justification for assignment changes where required.

Assignment history supports traceability and helps distinguish current responsibility from previous responsibility.

Changes to patient assignments must be authorized and validated by the backend.

## 13. Coordination and Reporting

Coordination reporting provides operational information to authorized users.

The reporting functionality may include:

- Patient counts.
- Appointment counts.
- Session counts.
- Canceled appointments.
- Appointment cancellation rates.
- Filtering by date range.
- Filtering by psychologist.

The interpretation of each metric depends on the underlying query, the selected reporting period, and the status definitions used by the application.

Reports must apply appropriate authorization and filtering rules to prevent disclosure of clinical or operational information to unauthorized users.

Operational reporting should not be interpreted as a clinical effectiveness evaluation unless a separate methodology supports that conclusion.

## 14. Authentication and Session Management

The application uses JWT-based authentication and database-backed refresh-token management.

Security-related functionality includes:

- Authentication before protected operations.
- Access-token validation.
- Refresh-token lifecycle management.
- User status validation.
- Role and permission checks.
- Server-side authorization for protected endpoints.

Frontend navigation may adapt to the authenticated user's permissions, but backend authorization remains the authoritative security boundary.

## 15. Data Protection

The system handles sensitive clinical information and applies security controls intended to reduce unauthorized disclosure.

Relevant controls include:

- Server-side access control.
- Assignment-aware patient authorization.
- Encryption of designated sensitive database fields.
- HTTPS for external traffic.
- Private document storage.
- Managed cloud secrets.
- Restricted AWS IAM permissions.

The effectiveness of these controls depends on correct implementation, configuration, testing, and operational monitoring.

No claim of regulatory compliance should be made without a corresponding assessment of the applicable requirements and deployed environment.

## 16. Feature Verification

Before describing a feature as complete, verify the relevant implementation and behavior.

| Area | Suggested verification |
| --- | --- |
| Patient management | Create, retrieve, update, and authorize patient operations. |
| Clinical history | Persist and retrieve clinical history while enforcing access restrictions. |
| Appointments | Verify status transitions, validation, and reporting behavior. |
| Clinical sessions | Verify patient relationships, ownership, and completion behavior. |
| Psychological tests | Verify catalog retrieval and any implemented evaluation workflows. |
| Document management | Verify upload, retrieval, and restricted access to stored documents. |
| Diagnostic catalog | Verify category relationships and active diagnosis filtering. |
| CDSS | Test matching rules, nonmatching rules, and professional confirmation behavior. |
| Concordance | Verify comparisons against the original suggestion and confirmed diagnosis. |
| Patient assignments | Test assignment, reassignment, history, and unauthorized access. |
| Coordination reports | Verify date filters, psychologist filters, and metric calculations. |
| Authentication | Test valid, expired, and invalid tokens. |
| Authorization | Test access by role, permission, and patient assignment. |
